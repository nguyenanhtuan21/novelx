# Takedown stops distribution without touching the record

ADR-0003 makes a Published Snapshot immutable so that NovelX can always say what readers saw. Two operations pull against that: a post-publication fix, which has to change the public text, and a takedown, which has to stop readers opening it at all. Doing either by writing to the snapshot would trade away the only property the snapshot exists for.

So a fix is a further version and a takedown is a record beside the snapshots. Neither writes to what was published. `published_snapshots` still has no update and no delete, and `chapter_takedowns` holds no content — the two write paths that would have destroyed evidence are the two that do not exist.

A revision carries the reason and the snapshot it replaced, on itself. The Staff Audit Record keeps the same reason as an operation, and the Provenance Ledger keeps a line for the change, but a reader asking "why is this text different from the one I remember" is asking about the snapshot, and an answer that lives only in a log beside it is one join away from being lost. `revision` is absent on a first publication because a first publication replaced nothing: an empty reason there would be a field nobody could fill honestly.

A fix goes back through everything. Rewriting a draft's prose takes its Quality Gate result and its Human Approval off it — the rule attaching material already followed (ADR-0018), for the same reason — so the fix that reaches the publishing door is prose a checker judged and a reviewer signed off. This closes the gap ADR-0018 named: editing a draft's prose had no route at all, which meant "a post-publication fix" had nothing to fix with. It has one now, and it costs the draft its approval exactly as attaching material does.

Takedown is its own permission. `chapter:takedown` is separate from `chapter:publish`, because taking content away from readers answers to different people than putting it there: a legal complaint or a policy breach is not an editorial decision, and an operator who can publish should not be able to make a Chapter disappear on their own. Repeating a takedown changes nothing, like repeating an approval or a Story Bible lock — the record names who took the decision, and a second attempt must not move that to whoever came last.

A takedown is keyed by Chapter, not by snapshot. Taking down version two and leaving version one distributable would republish older text nobody re-approved, which is the opposite of what a takedown is for. For the same reason, revising a Chapter under takedown is refused rather than quietly producing a version nobody can read: publishing a further version would otherwise be a way around a decision somebody else holds the permission for. Bringing a Chapter back is not modelled — nothing in this ticket asked for it, and a restore is a decision with its own accountability rather than a takedown with a field flipped.

What readers can open and what the Series has published are now read apart. Sequence is a fact about the Series that a takedown does not undo, so `publishedChapterNumbers` counts every Chapter ever published: otherwise taking Chapter one down would put every Chapter behind it out of sequence, and a moderation decision would freeze the release schedule. The reader-facing reads — the catalog and the public Chapter route — ask `listDistributedChapters` and `findDistributedChapter` instead, which is the question about now.

`publiclyReadable` is gone from the Published Snapshot, and `publicly_readable` from its table. It was a field on the snapshot claiming the snapshot was public, and a takedown makes that claim false while leaving the snapshot immutable — the flag out of step with what readers can open that `PublishedCatalogRepository` already argues against for Series. Distribution is read from the takedown record, in one place, by the reads that are about distribution. The dead `series.takedown_state` column goes with it: nothing ever wrote to it, and it made the SQL catalog hide Series the in-memory one could not, which is the disagreement between two deployments that a column with no term in the domain produces. Chapter Takedown is the term, and both catalogs now read it.

Staff inspect all of this through the publication record: every version of a Chapter, newest first, each naming the Staff Account that published it, the lineage it traced, and — from the second version on — why it replaced the one before, alongside the takedown if there is one. It reads and never filters, which is what makes it evidence rather than a view.
