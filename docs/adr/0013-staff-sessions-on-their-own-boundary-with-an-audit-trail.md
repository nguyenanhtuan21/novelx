# Staff sessions on their own boundary with an audit trail

Core Platform authenticates Staff Accounts on a staff boundary of their own: a staff session token signed with a staff signing secret separate from the reader one, presented on its own `X-Staff-Authorization: Staff <token>` header, carrying the permissions the Staff Account may act on, and running out after a short session window. Neither boundary accepts the other's token, so a Reader Account or Anonymous Reader Session can never reach a staff operation, whatever it presents. Every staff operation goes through one permission gate and leaves a record of actor, action, target, outcome, and time in an append-only staff audit trail — refused attempts included, so a reader session probing the staff boundary leaves evidence rather than silence.

What a staff session token names is the account, not what that account may do: permissions are read from the Staff Account directory on every request, so removing an account or taking a permission away takes effect on the next request instead of when the last session issued to it runs out.

For the MVP, Staff Accounts are provisioned through configuration: `STAFF_ACCOUNTS` lists each account's id, permissions, and the SHA-256 of a high-entropy access credential handed to an operator out of band. A deployment that configures nothing has no staff at all, which keeps local runs and the reader-facing surface free of accidental privilege. This is deliberately the smallest credible staff credential, not the final one: an identity provider, MFA, and step-up authentication for sensitive actions replace the directory behind the same boundary without moving it, and the audit trail already carries the shape those controls need. Modelling this split now, rather than after authorization logic spreads, is what ADR-0004 committed the project to.
