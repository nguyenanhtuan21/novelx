# Rights are checked where material enters a workflow

ADR-0007 requires a Rights Record before assets, datasets, references, or source materials are used in AI or publishing workflows. It does not say where that requirement is enforced, and the obvious place is the wrong one.

The gate therefore sits where material is attached to a workflow, not at the publishing door. Refusing to publish is no remedy for an AI workflow that has already read an unlicensed dataset: the use has happened, and the exposure is the use. So material enters a workflow only through a check that names the use — AI or publishing — and finds a Rights Record covering it. For the MVP the workflow is the one carrying a Draft Chapter, so material is attached to the draft; the attachment records which Rights Record cleared it, which is what a later Quality Gate and Provenance Ledger entry will read rather than re-deriving.

The check answers the rights question and nothing else, so it asserts no permission. An AI Factory workflow asking whether material is cleared must get the honest answer, not an authorization refusal, because a workflow that cannot ask cannot comply. Authority is asserted separately and at the staff boundary: `rights:write` to record a grant, `chapter:write` to attach material to a draft. This is deliberately unlike ADR-0014, where the canon gate lives in the domain because canon is human-owned — rights clearance is not owned by anyone, it is a fact about a licence.

Evidence is a grant somebody gave. Public availability and a source URL are modelled as claims and refused by name, rather than left out of the type, so that presenting one produces the reason instead of an unrelated validation error. That refusal is the whole point of the record: the failure mode this guards against is not an editor with no evidence, it is an editor with a URL who believes it is evidence.

A grant's scope of use and its AI-use right are kept as separate fields, and AI use is checked against both. A licence that permits publishing while forbidding model training is the ordinary case, and reading that off a single field would make the unsafe answer the easy one to reach by accident. A record claiming AI-workflow scope while refusing AI use is refused when it is written, in the domain and again in the database, because storing the contradiction would leave it to be reconciled by whoever read it next.

Material may be covered by more than one Rights Record — publishing under one contract, AI use under another — so the lookup lists every grant covering the material and the use is refused only when none of them covers it. A missing record and an exceeded grant are answered as different refusals: nobody checked is a different thing for an editor to act on than the licence says no.
