# Access reads Entitlement state, not payment-provider state

The spec models Entitlement from the start, even though real payment-provider integration is deferred until the core reader, CMS, publishing, and AI workflow flows stabilize. The question this ADR answers is what a reader-facing read checks to decide access, given that payment is not there yet: it reads the Entitlement the reader holds, and never the state of a payment provider that does not exist.

So a Chapter may be marked as demanding a benefit — early access or ad-free reading — and the catalog read asks `decideChapterAccess` whether the reader holds an entitlement for that Chapter whose benefit is the one it demands. A Chapter with no requirement is open to every reader, anonymous included, exactly as it was before. One that demands a benefit is granted only by a matching entitlement; a reader who does not hold one — or an Anonymous Reader Session that cannot — gets a 402 carrying the benefit and the content, so a client renders an upgrade-ready state from the refusal alone rather than a second read. One benefit does not stand in for another, because ad-free reading and early access answer different questions, and an entitlement for another Chapter answers none.

The requirement is held apart from the Published Snapshot, because access policy is a commercial decision that changes independently of the immutable content a snapshot keeps (ADR-0003). Adding a column to `published_snapshots` would put a mutable commercial field on a record whose purpose is to be unchangeable, and a snapshot that gained an entitlement gate nobody thought about is the same shape of accident the snapshot exists to prevent. `chapter_entitlement_requirements` is its own table, so a Chapter's access policy changing — including one being dropped to open the Chapter again — never touches what was published; an absent row is an open Chapter rather than a flag flipped back.

Granting an entitlement is the stub a payment provider's webhook would eventually call. Real integration with MoMo, VNPAY, Stripe, or any other PSP is out of scope for this ticket, so granting is a Staff Account operation behind `entitlement:write`, which writes a row a provider would have written. The access check reads that row and nothing else: it does not call a payment provider, does not read a subscription state, and does not infer entitlement from a purchase history. When a provider arrives, it writes here, and the read path does not change.

Marking and granting change access policy rather than content, so each leaves the Staff Audit Record and not the Provenance Ledger — like a schedule, an entitlement decision is an operation rather than lineage. `entitlement:write` is its own permission, separate from `chapter:publish` and `chapter:takedown`, because setting who may read a Chapter answers to a different authority than putting it in front of readers or taking it away: a commercial decision is not an editorial one, and an operator who can publish should not be able to gate a Chapter on their own.
