# The Quality Gate blocks on unanswered conditions

ADR-0001 makes human approval the public-publishing gate, and CONTEXT.md makes the Quality Gate multi-condition. Neither says what the gate does about a condition nobody answered, and that is the decision that matters most, because the ordinary way an unreviewed chapter reaches readers is not a check that failed — it is a check nobody made.

So an unanswered condition is a blocking failure. A run that reports nothing has checked nothing, and a gate that read silence as a pass could be passed by sending an empty body. The cost is that every run has to say what was checked, which is the point: the gate is a record of checks made, not a formality a workflow clears on its way past.

The conditions are answered from two places, and the split is the safety property. Canon and continuity, policy safety, originality and IP, and metadata are judgements about the prose that NovelX cannot reach by reading its own records, so they arrive as reported checks attributed to whoever ran the gate. Rights, provenance, and human approval are facts on record, so the gate looks them up itself and **refuses** a reported check that names one of them. Accepting such a report would be the gate believing its caller about exactly the things it exists to check — a workflow could then declare its own rights clearance.

Readiness is read off the blocking failures and nothing else. A run carries the mean of the scores that were reported, because an editor asking how a chapter is doing wants it, but no summing of scores can make a blocked chapter ready: the mean is computed after the verdicts and never consulted. It is named `meanReportedScore` rather than an aggregate quality score, which CONTEXT.md rules out as vocabulary precisely because a single number standing for the gate is the thing this decision refuses. That is what "a blocking failure prevents publication even if other scores are high" has to mean in code rather than in prose.

Human approval is a condition of the gate rather than a flag beside it, so a draft nobody has approved is not publicly publishable even when every content check passed. Recording an approval belongs to the publishing ticket, so today the best outcome a run can reach is every condition green with `humanApproval` outstanding. That is the honest state of a chapter awaiting review, and it is what an editor should see.

The rights condition resolves what it can. Material attached to the draft's workflow was cleared by the rights gate on the way in (ADR-0015), so its grants are checked facts — but only for the use they were cleared for. An attachment cleared for AI-workflow use does not answer the publishing question, because a licence permitting model use while forbidding publication is the ordinary case ADR-0015 keeps scope and AI-use apart for, and reading one clearance as the other would undo that at the last gate. A Rights Record the draft names for itself was, until now, a bare string nobody resolved — the gap ADR-0015 recorded — and the gate now looks it up and refuses an id nobody holds or a grant that does not cover publishing. What remains unchecked is the territory and duration of that grant, because a chapter is not modelled as workflow material and there is no territory of use to check it against. Narrower than before, and named rather than assumed.

The provenance condition reads the ledger, not the draft. ADR-0016 warns that filling `ChapterDraft.provenanceLedgerEntryId` with the authoring entry would let a draft satisfy the provenance condition by having been written; the gate avoids that by asking the Provenance Ledger for the lineage of this draft under this Series, and refusing entries that trace anything else. What it establishes is that the ledger can say how the draft was made, not that the lineage covers the prose as it stands now: a draft edited after a run is not detected, and re-running the gate is how a result is brought up to date. Worth doing when the CMS records an edit as its own line of lineage.

Running the gate needs `chapter:quality-gate`, separately from `chapter:write`. Declaring what the content checks found is a different authority from writing the prose, and an accountable gate is one where those two can be held by different people. Reading a result needs `series:read`, because the draft it belongs to is already readable with it. The domain evaluation asserts no permission of its own, like the rights gate and unlike the canon gate: whether a draft passes is a fact about records and reported checks, and authority over it is asked at the staff boundary.

A run appends a line of lineage naming what it concluded — the conditions that blocked and whether the draft is publicly publishable — because evaluation is one of the things the ledger exists to keep, and an entry that recorded only that a run happened would be indistinguishable from the next one. The result itself is held on the draft, where a later run replaces it; the ledger keeps what each run found.
